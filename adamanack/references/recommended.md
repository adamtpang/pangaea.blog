# Recommended aphorism reading

*Books that share Poor Adam's Almanack's lineage — practical, compressed, time-tested wisdom. Studied for both substance and form. The four already in `references/` are the canonical four; this list extends it.*

## Five to acquire next

These are all in or adjacent to the public domain (most fully out of copyright). Free editions are findable on Project Gutenberg, Standard Ebooks, or Wikisource.

### 1. *Meditations* — Marcus Aurelius (~170 CE)
- The original cornerstone of practical aphorism. Roman emperor's private journal, never meant for publication.
- Voice: stoic, brutally honest with himself, repeats himself as memorization.
- For Tomas: the "I'll never give up. Death is at my back" line is straight Marcus.
- **Edition I'd grab:** Gregory Hays translation (Modern Library). Most readable.

### 2. *Maxims* — La Rochefoucauld (1665)
- 504 numbered French aphorisms, each a single sentence. Cynical, courtly, devastating.
- The compression ceiling — most are under 15 words.
- For Tomas: shows what 100% aphorism + zero filler looks like at book scale. No theme names; just numbers 1–504.
- **Edition:** Penguin Classics (Tancock). Or Penguin's pocket *Reflections on Human Nature*.

### 3. *The Waste Books* (Aphorisms) — Georg Christoph Lichtenberg (1765–1799)
- German polymath's notebooks. Some lines pre-date Nietzsche, some out-think Twitter.
- Voice: skeptical, scientific, witty, unguarded.
- For Tomas: closest in temperament to your own — modern thinker writing for himself, posthumously beloved.
- **Edition:** NYRB Classics (R.J. Hollingdale). Beautiful object, beautifully translated.

### 4. *Hagakure* — Yamamoto Tsunetomo (~1716)
- Samurai aphorisms, dictated and compiled. Bushido in fragments.
- Voice: terse, unsentimental, often paradoxical ("The way of the samurai is found in death.")
- For Tomas: Pacific kinship — Japan is a stone's throw from Guam, the brevity is cousin to Hagåtña concision. Sets up "I'll never give up. Death is at my back" with deep cultural ancestry.
- **Edition:** William Scott Wilson translation (Shambhala). The Tsuramoto compilation, not the long version.

### 5. *Tao Te Ching* — Lao Tzu (~6th c. BCE)
- 81 short verses. The original compression.
- For Tomas: "keep subtracting, and what is left is the answer" is functionally a Tao Te Ching line. Worth reading just to see how few words it takes to say something true.
- **Edition:** Stephen Mitchell or Ursula K. Le Guin. Le Guin's is more poetic; Mitchell's is the popular standard.

## Honorable mentions (deeper bench)

- *Pensées* — Blaise Pascal (~1670). Christian apologetics in fragments. Less practical, more philosophical, but the form is exemplary.
- *Essays* — Francis Bacon (1597). "Of Studies," "Of Truth" — Bacon writing in 16th-century English aphorism is just *good*.
- *Aphorisms* — Arthur Schopenhauer (1851, in *Parerga and Paralipomena*). German pessimism in compressed form.
- *Beyond Good and Evil* / *The Gay Science* — Friedrich Nietzsche. Section 4 of BGE is pure aphorism.
- *Letters from a Stoic* — Seneca. Letters ARE long, but each contains aphorisms; Penguin's "Stoic Reflections" pulls the best.
- *The Analects* — Confucius. Chinese practical wisdom; pairs with Lao Tzu.
- *The Book of Five Rings* — Miyamoto Musashi (1645). Strategy aphorisms from a swordsman.
- *The Discourses* — Epictetus. More practical than Marcus, less polished. Aphorism-adjacent.
- *Tools of Titans* — Tim Ferriss (2016). Companion to *Tribe of Mentors*. Bigger, less curated.
- *Daily Stoic* — Ryan Holiday (2016). Stoic aphorisms with day-by-day commentary. Modern packaging.

## Books we actively want to **not** copy

- Encyclopedias of mental models (Charlie's Almanack tier). Substance is gold; format isn't ours.
- Self-help anthologies arranged by life advice category. The aphorism stands alone — we don't need to caption it.
- Anything with a foreword by a celebrity that runs longer than the longest aphorism.

---

## How this list shapes the work

- **Length floor:** Lichtenberg and La Rochefoucauld average ~12–18 words. Our aphorisms should sit there too.
- **Numbering:** La Rochefoucauld's continuous 1–504 is our model (continuous, themes don't reset).
- **Interludes:** Tribe of Mentors' "Quotes I'm Pondering" between mentor groups is the template we used. Marcus's habit of quoting earlier philosophers is the same instinct.
- **Edge:** Procrustes and Lichtenberg both prove that 5–10 words can carry a paradox. Our aphorisms should occasionally bite, not only soothe.
- **Restraint:** All five recommended books, in their best editions, are **typographic only on the cover.** No imagery. We hold that line.
