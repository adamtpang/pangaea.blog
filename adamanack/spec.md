# The Adamanack — Design Spec

*The constitution for Poor Adam's Almanack. Every decision in the project gets held against this document. If a feature isn't in here, it's not in the book.*

---

## Mission

A small, beautiful, timeless book of original aphorisms — every line carved-over-a-doorway worthy — distilled from a decade of Tomas's published and drafted writing across X (posts + drafts), Farcaster (casts + drafts), and essay drafts. **Minimal in form, maximal in compression.** Read in one sitting, returned to for life.

## Form principles

1. **One section, not five.** The current draft has five (Aphorisms, Field Notes, Calendar, Builder's Almanac, Marginalia). Cut to one. The book is aphorisms. Everything else is a junk drawer that dilutes the average.
2. **150–200 final lines.** Franklin printed ~900 over 25 years; the cultural survivors are ~60. You publish ~175 — every one polished to survive.
3. **Organize by 7–12 themes**, decided *after* curation, not before. Themes emerge from what you actually wrote. Likely candidates given your work: building, attention, friendship, money, time, the body, taste, foolishness, death, work, language, memory.
4. **Numbered, like Franklin.** Continuous numbering across the whole book (1 through ~175). Themes are headers; numbers don't reset. This honors the tradition and gives every line a permanent address.
5. **No essays. No field notes. No calendar.** Those are V2 fuel. The aphorism alone is the unit.
6. **Voice register: modern, with one foot in the tradition.** Plain English. Occasional formal cadence ("Be slow to…"). No fake "thee" or "thou." No emoji, no hashtags, no internet residue.

---

## Substance principles (the carved-doorway test)

Every line must pass three tests before it ships:

1. **Stands alone.** No context required. Strip the tweet/cast/sentence from its origin and it still works.
2. **Compresses.** Could you cut a word? If yes, cut it. Aim for ≤ 20 words. Most should land between 8 and 15.
3. **Earns memorability.** A reader could quote it from memory after one read. If not — close, but cut.

**The 80% deletion rule.** If your raw corpus yields 1,000 candidates, your final cut is ~175. If you can't bear to cut 80%, you're not curating, you're hoarding.

## Aesthetic principles (steal directly from Naval, not Franklin's reprint)

1. **Page size:** 5" × 8" hardcover dimensions (digital matches). Pocket-sized. Holdable.
2. **Typography:** ONE serif. Candidates: EB Garamond, Sabon, Caslon, Centaur. Body 11–12pt, leading generous (1.5–1.6×).
3. **One aphorism per page.** That is the entire aesthetic move. Massive whitespace. Number top-left in small caps. Aphorism centered or top-aligned. Nothing else.
4. **Section openers:** a single page with the theme name in lowercase, hairline rule above and below. Nothing else.
5. **Cover:** typographic only. Cream stock, black ink. Title in a single elegant serif. Subtitle one line. Byline. No imagery, no ornament. Franklin would approve. Naval did this.
6. **No illustrations. No ornaments. No drop caps. No callout boxes.** Anything decorative is a tell that you didn't trust the words.

## What this book is NOT (the anti-spec)

- ❌ Not a memoir
- ❌ Not a collection of essays
- ❌ Not a builder's manual
- ❌ Not seasonal / monthly / calendrical
- ❌ Not a journal with dated entries
- ❌ Not a reference book for ideas you haven't yet had
- ❌ Not a vehicle for blog drafts or unwritten posts (those go in `parking-lot.md`)
- ❌ Not 600 pages
- ❌ Not illustrated

If at any point you're tempted to add one of these, you've lost the thread. Re-read this section.

## Front and back matter (everything else is cut)

**Front:**
1. Title page (title, subtitle, byline, year)
2. Dedication (one line)
3. Preface (300 words MAX — why, the Franklin nod, the reader's contract)
4. Contents (themes only, no aphorism listing)

**Back:**
1. Author's note (one page — sources: a decade of public thought distilled)
2. Colophon (set in [typeface], produced [year/place])

That's it. No index, no bibliography, no acknowledgments page that runs three pages.

## The persona question

**Recommendation: drop "Poor Adam's Almanack" → reconsider.**

The "Poor X's" framing was Franklin's joke — Richard Saunders was a fictional pauper-astrologer narrating the book. Charlie's title was a tribute to Franklin (Munger's hero). Naval dropped the "Poor" entirely.

You have two clean options:
- **Tribute mode:** *Poor Adam's Almanack* — owns the inheritance, but invites direct comparison with Franklin (a steep bar). Your byline could be a persona ("Adam") with your real name in small type.
- **Naval mode:** *The Almanack of Tomas Pangelinan* — modern, owns the work, no persona scaffolding. Lets the lines stand on their own without the Franklin lens.

**Working title for now:** *Poor Adam's Almanack* (you've already named it). Decide for real before Phase 7 (Frame).

## How we'll know it's 10/10

A 10/10 almanack passes all six of these on launch day:

1. **Compression test.** Average aphorism is ≤ 15 words. No filler.
2. **Standalone test.** Open to any random page; the line works without context.
3. **Memorability test.** Three friends, each given a copy, can quote a different aphorism a week later without re-reading.
4. **Aesthetic test.** A stranger picks it off a shelf and says "this looks important."
5. **Re-read test.** You read it cover-to-cover yourself in one sitting and don't wince.
6. **Loop-closure test.** It exists as a finished, shippable artifact. Done beats perfect; *but minimal makes done easier than perfect.*
