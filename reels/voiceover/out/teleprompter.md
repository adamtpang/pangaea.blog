# Teleprompter script


## ACT 1 — The Story Everyone Knows

**1.** On October 29, 1929, "Black Tuesday" hit Wall Street as investors traded some 16 million shares on the New York Stock Exchange in a single day. Around $14 billion of stock value was lost, wiping out thousands of investors.

**2.** At the height of the Depression in 1933, 24.9% of the total work force or 12,830,000 people was unemployed.

**3.** The U.S. did not return to 1929 GNP for over a decade and still had an unemployment rate of about 15% in 1940, albeit down from the high of 25% in 1933.

**4.** With the creation of 17 million new jobs during the war, workers were afforded the opportunity to pay off old debts, as well as to begin saving some of their earnings.

**5.** Every American automaker turned its workforce and facilities to military production during World War II. But no project captured the public's imagination like Willow Run, where Ford Motor Company built one B-24 Liberator airplane every 63 minutes.

**6.** Defence spending rose from 1.4% of GDP in 1940 to over 37% in 1945 and the federal deficit rose from 3% of GDP in 1939 to 27.5% in 1943.

**7.** In 1944, unemployment dipped to 1.2 percent of the civilian labor force, a record low in American economic history and as near to "full employment" as is likely possible.


## ACT 2 — The Illusion (Wartime Rationing & Post-War Transition)

**8.** This led to rationing of meat, gasoline, fuel oil, kerosene, nylon, silk, shoes, sugar, coffee, processed foods, cheese, and milk.

**9.** Price Administrator Leon Henderson ordered Detroit to cease making cars and trucks for the civilian market in order to free up assembly lines for military production.

**10.** During the war the personal saving rate had risen to extraordinary levels: 23.6 percent in 1942, 25.0 percent in 1943, 25.5 percent in 1944, and 19.7 percent in 1945. After the war the personal saving rate fell to 9.5 percent in 1946 and 4.3 percent in 1947.

**11.** Once the consumption figures are adjusted to develop better estimates of the true prices, the amount consumed per person was lower throughout the war than it was in 1940 when the economy was still climbing out of the Great Depression.

**12.** Obviously during this process there will be a great deal of inevitable unemployment.

**13.** Paul Samuelson, a future Nobel Prize winner, wrote in 1943 that upon cessation of hostilities and demobilization "some ten million men will be thrown on the labor market." He warned there would be "the greatest period of unemployment and industrial dislocation which any economy has ever faced."

**14.** Between mid-1945 and mid-1947, over 20 million people were released from the armed forces and related employment, but nonmilitary-related civilian employment rose by 16 million. The unemployment rate rose from 1.9 percent to just 3.9 percent.

**15.** This was described by President Truman as "the swiftest and most gigantic change-over that any nation has made from war to peace."


## ACT 3 — The Room Where It Happened (Gold Standard & Bretton Woods)

**16.** Structural economic imbalances resulting from World War I and the restoration of the gold standard greatly increased the fragility of the international monetary and financial system.

**17.** A loaf of bread in Berlin that cost around 160 marks at the end of 1922 cost 200 billion marks by late 1923.

**18.** There was a time when an average German carried billions of marks in their pockets but could still buy nothing. A loaf of bread cost 200 billion marks. A week's pension would not buy even a cup of coffee.

**19.** Before World War II, nations had imposed crippling trade barriers and engaged in competitive devaluations of their currencies. Such "beggar-thy-neighbor" policies were blamed for exacerbating the global economic downturn.

**20.** Overall, world trade declined by some 66% between 1929 and 1934.

**21.** A new international monetary system was forged by delegates from forty-four nations in Bretton Woods, New Hampshire, in July 1944.

**22.** The countries agreed to keep their currencies fixed but adjustable, within a 1 percent band, to the dollar, and the dollar was fixed to gold at $35 an ounce.

**23.** Contrary to early predictions, this high growth also included many countries that had been devastated by the war, such as Japan, West Germany and Austria, South Korea, Belgium, France, Italy and Greece.


## ACT 4 — The Break (Nixon, De Gaulle, End of Bretton Woods)

**24.** In France, it was called America's exorbitant privilege, as it resulted in an asymmetric financial system where foreigners saw themselves supporting American living standards and subsidizing American multinationals.

**25.** On March 19, 1968, President Johnson signed a bill eliminating the gold cover, the reserve backing by gold, for Federal Reserve notes.

**26.** The Bank of France publicly announced a conversion of $300 million dollars into gold, 267 tonnes, in January 1965, followed by the General's press conference on February 4th, where he advocated for a return to the gold standard.

**27.** All of France's dollars were converted into gold, and to avoid treachery, the metal was repatriated over the course of three years. It took 44 boat trips and 129 flights to bring home more than three thousand tonnes of gold to the Banque de France in Paris.

**28.** In the current system, the United States can go into debt for free, at the expense of other countries, because what the United States owes them from trade is paid, at least in part, with dollars only they can create.

**29.** In May 1971, West Germany left the Bretton Woods system, unwilling to sell further Deutschmarks for U.S. dollars. On August 11th, Britain requested $3 billion in gold be moved from Fort Knox to the Federal Reserve in New York.

**30.** On the afternoon of Friday, August 13th, 1971, Nixon, Burns, Connally, Volcker, and twelve other high-ranking White House and Treasury advisors met secretly at Camp David to discuss policy solutions to the growing crisis.

**31.** Nixon directed Connally to suspend the convertibility of the dollar into gold or other reserve assets, such that foreign governments could no longer exchange their dollars for gold, thereby ending the Bretton Woods system.

**32.** The new record of $5,104 followed previous milestones achieved just in the past year as gold broke the $3,000 and $4,000 marks in March and October of 2025.

**33.** The Nixon shock has been widely considered to be a political success but an economic failure, for bringing on the 1973 to 1975 recession, the stagflation of the 1970s, and the instability of floating currencies.

**34.** There is no subtler, no surer means of overturning the existing basis of society than to debauch the currency. The process engages all the hidden forces of economic law on the side of destruction, and does it in a manner which not one man in a million is able to diagnose.
