Curation is arranging what already exists: picking the right playlist, the right reading list, the right five projects out of fifty options. It's low risk and it works immediately, because you're selecting from things already proven to be good. That's why curation makes a stable floor: it rarely fails badly, it just sometimes fails to be original.

Creation is making something that didn't exist before, and it has no floor at all. It can fail completely, cost real time, and go nowhere. But it's the only source of anything genuinely new, because curation, by definition, can only ever rearrange what's already out there.

The mistake is picking a side permanently. All curation and you're stable but static, a very well organized collection of other people's ideas. All creation and you're dynamic but exhausted, throwing away the floor every single day. The actual move is sequencing: curate to build the stable base that pays the bills and buys the time, create on top of that floor, where a swing that doesn't land can't take the whole structure down with it.
