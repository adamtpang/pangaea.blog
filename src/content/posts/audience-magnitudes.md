Every audience has a magnitude, and the magnitude sets the speed, more than the people in it do. A room of ten can change its mind in an afternoon. A room of ten thousand takes weeks, if it ever agrees on anything at all. Scale doesn't just add people, it adds coordination cost, and coordination cost is what actually determines how fast a group can move.

This is why small rooms ship faster than large ones at every magnitude: a founding team beats a department, a group chat beats a public forum, a garage beats a committee. Feedback in a small room is immediate and specific, from people who have real context. Feedback at scale is slow, generic, and arrives filtered through everyone else's opinion of the opinion, by the time it reaches you it's been through several rounds of dilution.

So audience size isn't just a vanity question, it's an operating-speed question, which is the real reason to [[Get To The Group Chat]] instead of optimizing the public feed. If you need to iterate fast, you want the small magnitude, even if it costs you reach. The big room is for distribution, once the thing already works. Build in the small room. Announce in the big one.
