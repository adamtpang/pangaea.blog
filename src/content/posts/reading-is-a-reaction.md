Nobody picks up a book at random, even when it feels that way. Some question was already forming, some gap already open, and the book that finally gets pulled off the shelf is the one that happens to answer it. You didn't choose to read about grief because you were bored. You chose it because something in you was already asking.

This means a reading list is a kind of diary written backward. Look at what you actually finished, not what you meant to, over the last year, and you'll see the real questions you were carrying, more honestly than a journal would tell you, because you didn't choose the books to look good to yourself later.

It also means the advice to "read more broadly" slightly misses the point. The reaction is the useful part. What you're drawn to right now is data about what's actually unresolved in you right now, and following that pull, rather than a curated list of what you should be interested in, is usually the faster way to whatever you're actually looking for.
