I don't have investors or an employer. What I have is apps, essays, music, and websites, and whoever finds any of it useful enough to pay for keeps it running. That's the whole model: patron-funded, not venture-funded. No board to report to, no boss deciding what I work on next.

The trade is obvious once you say it out loud. No investor money means no runway handed to you up front, no big check that lets you ignore whether anyone actually wants the thing yet. Every project has to earn its own keep or it doesn't continue, which is a harder floor than a funded team gets, but a more honest one.

What it buys back is alignment. Nobody I answer to wants a different outcome than the person actually using the work. No investor pushing for a pivot that serves a return timeline instead of the people I made it for. The freedom isn't romantic, it's structural: when the incentive of the person paying and the incentive of the person building are the same incentive, you get to build the thing you'd build anyway.
