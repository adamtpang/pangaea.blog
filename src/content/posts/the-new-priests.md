A priest used to be the person who told you how to live: what to eat, when to suffer, how to want the right things. Most people don't have that figure anymore in the literal sense, so a new one filled the vacancy: the podcast host with a protocol, the psychologist with a set of rules for being a man, the long-form interview you queue up like a sermon.

The function transferred even though the building didn't. People still want someone credentialed and confident to tell them what's true and how to behave, and they'll take that from a microphone now instead of a pulpit, sometimes with better evidence, sometimes with worse, always with the same hunger underneath.

That hunger isn't naive. It's just unmet the old way and searching for a new one. Worth noticing who currently holds the office, and asking the same question you'd ask any priest: what are they actually selling, and does it hold up outside the sermon.
