My thinking is high signal now, and not noisy, and I can name exactly what did it: David Deutsch, David Senra, and psychedelics. Three very different sources, one shared property: none of them were built to be interesting in the next five minutes. All three were built to still be true, or still be felt, years later.

Most feeds are the opposite. Optimized to be interesting, not true or useful, which means the default diet is high volume and low signal: outrage, novelty, a hot take that won't matter in a week. You can consume for hours and end the day knowing less than when you started, just more agitated about it.

Deutsch gave me a theory of how knowledge actually grows. Senra gave me a hundred cases of what that theory looks like lived out in an actual founder's decisions. Psychedelics did something the other two couldn't: they didn't add information, they cleared the channel the information travels through, the noise that isn't content but static, the anxious loop that makes even good input hard to hear clearly.

None of the three are optimized to be shareable in five minutes, which is exactly the tell. Signal doesn't need to shout. Cut whatever fails that test, however productive it feels, and keep whatever doesn't.
