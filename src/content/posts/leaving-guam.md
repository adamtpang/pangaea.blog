I was born on Guam, a thirty-mile island where the world arrives on a delay: news, products, culture, all of it hitting somewhere else first. Leaving wasn't a single dramatic exit. It was a slow accumulation of moves, App Academy in 2022 the clearest hinge point, each one putting more distance between me and the island where everything happened to someone else first.

Leaving costs something real, and it's not the thing people assume. It's not the place itself, which you can always visit. It's the specific, unearned closeness of a small community, the version of belonging that doesn't require choosing anyone, because you were all just born in the same small place together.

What it buys is a different kind of belonging, one you actually pick. I ended up longtermer number two at Network School in Langkawi, another island, almost the same size, and the joke took me a while to notice: I left an island to live on an island. The difference was never the geography. It's that this one is full on purpose, of people who chose to be in the same place at the same time, deliberately, which is the exact thing that never happens to you by accident.
