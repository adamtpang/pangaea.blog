Ambition is a hot fuel and a dirty one. It gets you out of bed at five, gets the first version shipped, survives the years nobody else believed in the thing, but it burns with a lot of smoke: resentment when it's not rewarded fast enough, comparison, a version of you that's a little unpleasant to be around when the metrics dip. It works, but it works like gasoline works, fast and violent and hard on the engine.

Love burns at a lower temperature and doesn't run out the same way. It doesn't need the win to keep going, doesn't need anyone watching, doesn't curdle into resentment when the timeline slips, because it was never conditional on the outcome in the first place. It's a worse starter fuel and a better one for the actual distance.

The mistake is picking one instead of sequencing them. Ambition to get the engine running, love to keep it running once the smoke clears and the early adrenaline stops being enough on its own. Most burnout is just ambition still trying to do a job that only love was ever built to finish.
