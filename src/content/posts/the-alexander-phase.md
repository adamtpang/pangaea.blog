I can trace my own thinking through the same lineage. There was a Socrates phase: mostly questioning, poking at everything, more interested in finding the cracks in an idea than building anything on top of it. Then a Plato phase: taking the best questions and trying to build a coherent theory out of them, a model of how things actually work. Then an Aristotle phase: organizing the theory into something teachable, systems and frameworks instead of loose insight.

None of those three, on their own, ever shipped anything. Aristotle tutored a teenager named Alexander, who took three generations of refined thought and went and did something with it, for better and for worse, and that's the phase I'm in now. Not because the questioning or the theorizing or the organizing stopped mattering, but because at some point the thinking has to spend itself on something real, or it was never anything but a very well organized hobby.

It's a legendary memetic lineage precisely because it's a lineage, not a single move. You don't skip to Alexander. You earn the action phase by actually doing the earlier ones first, badly, for a while. But you also don't get to stay in Aristotle forever just because organizing feels like progress. At some point you close the books and go conquer something.
