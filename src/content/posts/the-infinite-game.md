A finite game has an end and a winner: a match, a quarter, an exit. An infinite game has no end, only players who stay in and players who drop out, and the entire objective is to keep playing, not to win a round of it. Most careers, most companies, most creative practices are actually infinite games that people insist on treating like finite ones.

Treat an infinite game like a finite one and you optimize for the wrong thing: the big launch instead of the next hundred launches, the viral post instead of the practice that makes viral posts a byproduct, the exit instead of the thing that was worth building regardless of an exit. You can win every finite round inside an infinite game and still lose the game, by burning out, running out of resources, or simply quitting once the scoreboard stops feeling exciting.

The tell is simple: ask whether you're optimizing to win this round or to still be a player next year. If the two answers point in different directions, you've probably picked the finite move. The infinite game rewards whoever is still at the table, not whoever had the best single hand.
