Vinyl is objectively worse than streaming. Heavier, more expensive, lower fidelity by most measures, and it demands you sit in one spot instead of taking the music with you. It survived anyway, and it survived precisely because it's worse in every efficient sense: the friction is the point. Putting the needle down is a ritual, not a task, and rituals are what turn consuming something into paying attention to it.

Physical books are the same trade. An ebook is lighter, searchable, infinitely stockable, and objectively the more efficient format. A physical book takes up shelf space, can't be searched, and weighs down a bag. It survives for the same reason vinyl did: the weight and the page-turning and the fact that you can't skim it at ten times speed are the mechanism, not the cost.

Neither format won on efficiency, because efficiency was never actually what people wanted from either one. They wanted the version of the experience that couldn't be rushed. The inefficient formats are the ones still standing precisely because everything efficient replaced the wrong thing: the delivery, not the attention.
