Companies are fictions. So is money. Useful fictions, ones millions of strangers agree to believe in so they can coordinate, but fictions all the same. Underneath them there's a real economy, and it isn't measured in dollars, it's measured in time per thing produced: how many man-hours it takes to make one loaf, one house, one working piece of software.

Lee Kuan Yew and Elon both understood this, in different domains, which is why both of them read as obsessive about the same unglamorous variable. Not growth for its own sake, not the score on a balance sheet, but the actual throughput: how much real output a given hour of human effort now produces, and whether that number is moving. LKY ran a country on it. Elon runs factories on it.

Money is a claim on that output, never the output itself. Push the time-per-thing number down for something people actually need, and you've made the world richer, whatever the price tag says afterward. Raise your price without touching that number and you've only rearranged who holds the claim.

This is a useful lens for your own hours too, not just companies and countries. Not "what does my time cost," but "what does an hour of mine currently produce, and can that number go up." That question is the actual economy. The bank account is just the scoreboard.
