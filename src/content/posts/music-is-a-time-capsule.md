A photo shows you a moment from the outside. A song puts you back inside one. Three seconds into a track you haven't heard in years and you're not remembering a summer, you're back in the car with the windows down, the actual feeling reassembling itself faster than thought, before you've consciously placed the year or the person you were with.

No other medium does this as reliably. Smell comes close, but you can't carry a smell in your pocket and press play on it later. Music is a time capsule you build without meaning to, one that stores not facts about a period of your life but the actual texture of being inside it, the mood before you had language for the mood.

Which is a reason to make music, not just consume it. Every song you finish is a capsule you're sealing for a future version of yourself, one who won't remember the specific afternoon you wrote it but will feel it again, whole, the moment the first chord hits. You're not just making something to be heard now. You're leaving something for later.
