The man who cracked open the Dutch spice trade was not good at his job.

Cornelis de Houtman left Amsterdam in 1595 with four ships and roughly 250 men. Scurvy broke out within weeks. He was undiplomatic enough that one ruler refused to deal with him at all, so he could not buy the spices he had crossed the world for. Fewer than 90 men came home, too weak to moor their own ships.

Within five years, 65 more Dutch ships had sailed that route. Within seven, the VOC existed.

He did not open the trade because he was exceptional. He opened it because he went, badly, first.

**Whatever you are looking up at was built by a human body running the same equipment as yours.** Same fear response. Same bad nights. Same ratio of stupid decisions to good ones. The difference is almost never a different kind of nervous system. It is that they kept going while the story was still ugly.

What fools us is compression. You meet great people already finished. The decade of nothing is one sentence in the introduction. The botched first attempt becomes charming foreshadowing. You are holding your live, unedited footage up against somebody else's final cut.

Read a real biography instead of a summary and this collapses fast. They were young, unqualified, and improvising. Exactly like you.

The ceiling is not superhuman. It is human, which means it is a height a human has already stood at, which means the question was never whether it can be reached.

Go badly, first.
