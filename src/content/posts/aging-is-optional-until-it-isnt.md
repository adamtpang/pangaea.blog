Most of what people call aging is a decision made daily to stop paying attention. Skip the walk, skip the new skill, skip the conversation that might change your mind, and something calcifies faster than any cell could manage on its own. That kind of aging is optional. You can refuse it at seventy the same way you can surrender to it at twenty five.

But there is a floor under the choice. Cells divide a finite number of times. Joints wear. Recovery slows whether you notice or not. The body keeps a ledger the mind cannot override by willpower alone. Pretend otherwise and you are not optimistic, you are just unprepared for the day the bill comes due, and denial is its own kind of aging, the fastest kind.

So the honest position sits between the two easy ones. Not "aging is inevitable, so accept it." Not "aging is a choice, so defeat it." Fight the part that is optional: curiosity, movement, appetite for the unfamiliar, for as long as you possibly can. Respect the part that is not, and plan for it instead of denying it exists.

The people who age worst are rarely the ones who lost the biological fight. They are the ones who quit the optional fight decades early, then blamed the floor for a fall they chose.

Youth is not a number. It is a rate of change you keep choosing to sustain, right up until the day biology finally gets a vote too.
