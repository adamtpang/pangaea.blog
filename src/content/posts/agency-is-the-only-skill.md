Agency Is the Only Skill.

Everything else has a tutorial. Guitar, French, Postgres, negotiating a lease: someone already drew the map, and the map is free. What no map gives you is the part where you open it today, unprompted, with nobody grading the result.

Watch two people with identical resumes. One waits for the assignment. The other notices the thing nobody has claimed, claims it, and hands back work no one asked for. Same skills, different decade. The gap is not intelligence, and it is not confidence, which arrives afterward anyway. It is the private belief that you are allowed.

Most of what looks like a skill gap is a permission gap. People do not fail to write to the person they admire because they cannot compose an email. They fail because somewhere along the way they absorbed that writing to them is not for people like us. School rehearses this for twelve years: you get excellent at other people's problems and never practice choosing your own. Then you graduate into a world that only rewards the second thing.

The good news is that agency trains like anything else, in reps too small to frighten you. Send the message. Ask the price. Publish the ugly draft. Say the number out loud. Start the band with the two people already in the room. Each rep teaches the same lesson: the door was never locked, it was only closed, and closed is a thing a hand can fix.

You will not run out of things to learn. There is more free instruction than you have hours. What you can run out of is years spent waiting to be picked.

Go get picked by yourself. The skills follow whoever went and got them.
