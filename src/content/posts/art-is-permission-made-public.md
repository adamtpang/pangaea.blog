Someone makes an ugly, honest, specific thing and puts it where strangers can see it. That is the whole mechanism. Nobody announces "you may now also do this." The permission is just the object existing, unapologized for.

This is why bad art matters as much as good art. A polished masterpiece proves talent exists somewhere; it does not prove you are allowed to try. A rough, weird, clearly-made-by-a-person thing proves the second thing, and the second thing is what actually moves someone from watching to making.

So publish before it is ready. The finished version teaches admiration. The unfinished version teaches permission, and permission is the scarcer resource.
