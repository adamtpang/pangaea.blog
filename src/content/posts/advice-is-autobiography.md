Advice Is Autobiography.

When someone hands you advice, listen for the wound underneath it. "Never trust a partner" is a divorce talking. "Save every penny" is a childhood without much. "Quit while you're ahead" is a person who once didn't, and paid. The advice is real, but it is a report from one life, not a law of all lives.

This is not a reason to ignore people. It is a reason to read them. The best question to ask any advice-giver is not "is this true?" but "true for whom, and after what?" The answer turns a command back into a story, and stories you can actually learn from. A rule tells you what to do. A story tells you what it cost.

It works on you too. The advice you give away most forcefully is the note you most need to read yourself. Watch which lessons you press on your friends with the heat still in them. That heat is not wisdom about them; it is unfinished business about you. We teach loudest where we are still bleeding.

So take advice the way you'd take a gift from a specific person's specific hands: gratefully, and with a glance at where they've been. Keep what fits your terrain. Leave what was shaped by a life you are not living.

And when you catch yourself insisting, hard, that someone must do the thing you'd do, stop and ask who you are really talking to. Often it is a younger version of you, in the row behind, who never got told. Say it to them. Then take your own advice for once.
