Doing something badly by accident feels like failure. Doing something badly on purpose is a different move entirely: a way of getting past the part of you that needs the first attempt to be good, by removing goodness from the goal before you start.

Write the ugly first draft on purpose. Play the wrong notes on purpose. Ship the version you're slightly embarrassed by, on purpose. Once bad is the explicit target, you can't fail at it, and the paralysis that comes from protecting a fragile "this needs to be good" disappears, because there was never anything to protect.

Most people's actual first drafts are already bad. The trick is choosing that on purpose instead of discovering it by accident, because chosen badness doesn't come with any shame attached.
